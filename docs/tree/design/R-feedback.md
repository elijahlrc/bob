# The tree rework: the owner's feedback (2026-10-31)

Collected from the owner's pass over the phone viewer of the unbaked tree, before a plan is written. Each item is the
owner's point, then what was measured.

## 1. Second notables hung off a notable

The owner, on Steadfast Faith (off Iron Faith) and Whirling Ward (off Warding Staff): an anti-pattern. It feels tacked
on, and it takes away a decision: everyone who takes the cluster's notable spends one more point on the second.

Measured: 40 such seconds across the regions (the generators' `second` tables), nearly all one step past the
cluster's notable with nothing else beyond them:

- the Spire 10;
- the Knife 8;
- the Yard 8;
- the Cathedral 7;
- the Bow 6;
- the Wall 1.

They were added in the notables rounds to raise the notable count without new clusters.

**Decided (the owner):** the notables stay. Each one gets a real place, in a new cluster or in an existing cluster
grown to make room for it. Folding, moving or cutting them was rejected.

## 2. The generalist V in front of every start

The owner, on Honed Steel in front of the Reaver: having this near every start is an anti-pattern. A good node nearly
everyone wants, that leads nowhere, makes three boring, near-mandatory picks that go nowhere.

Measured: every start has one, five steps out, at the bottom of a V between the start's two exits, with no way
onward. Five have two links and the Knife's has three:

- First Light (Mystic);
- Keen Instinct (Shade, three links);
- Hawk's Eye (Strider);
- Honed Steel (Reaver);
- Oathbound (Vanguard);
- Zeal (Zealot).

It follows from the skeleton's start and from a goal the rework set itself: a generalist notable at step 5 for every
class.

**Decided (the owner):** the pattern goes. A node everyone wants should lead somewhere.

## 3. Small-scale structure is the same everywhere

The owner: regions should be at least somewhat diverse in their small-scale structure. Having the same thing
everywhere is not ideal.

Measured: the skeleton gave every region the same small-scale forms:

- the same start, with two exits and a V;
- paths to the seams of two nodes through a home cluster;
- side rooms hung off roads by one entrance;
- seconds one step past a notable (item 1).

**Decided (the owner):** regions differ in their small-scale structure, at least somewhat.

## 4. Clusters as a way across the map

The owner: more clusters should come off related clusters or connect to other existing clusters. That makes the map
more interconnected and gives an alternative to the roads for getting around. Going through clusters is a good trade:
higher-value nodes, but more of them to reach where you are going than by road.

This answers the report's question 1 (loops or side rooms) and its third risk (railing): the side rooms hung off a road
by one entrance give way to clusters linked to related clusters, so routes through clusters stand beside the roads.
Measured before: about a third of the reference's loops within 15 steps of a start (29-44 against 90-111), and 4 to 6
links between regions per border.

**Decided (the owner):** more cluster-to-cluster links, both a cluster grown off a related one and links between
existing clusters. A route through clusters is the slower, richer alternative to a road.
