# B. What good trees have in common: the comparison

Phase B of [PASSIVE-TREE.md](../../PASSIVE-TREE.md), assembled 2026-10-19 from the five game studies in this folder
(`B-poe.md` measured from the 3.9 data; `B-last-epoch.md`, `B-diablo.md`, `B-grim-dawn.md`, `B-others.md` from the web,
each number marked there as sourced or recalled). One table: the property, its range across the games, and what Bob wants
and why. Only PoE's numbers drive a Bob number; the others give the direction and the counter-examples.

**Bob's situation, which the "why" column keeps coming back to:** a fresh character every run; 123 points over about 100
maps and 25 minutes at speed; the tree opened up to a hundred times a run for one to four points each, on a phone; the
first four points spent before the build's skill is known; no ascendancies, masteries or skill trees, so the tree carries
the whole identity; nobody controls the character in a fight, so a node is only real if the sheet can show it; and, from
the telemetry, the median run so far died on map 4 holding two points, nine in ten runs never held more than 22.

## What the five games agree on

1. **The first ten points are a tutorial.** Every game makes them safe and makes the first real choice visible and named.
2. **Travel is the cost players resent**, and every game but PoE removed it. The one with travel keeps it short (1–3), puts
   the plainest nodes at the junctions, and makes the attribute worth something.
3. **A cluster is one idea.** Smalls agree with their notable; a cluster is guessable from one word; it has one way in.
4. **Keystones are far, dead-ended and mostly trades.** The approach is a commitment to a theme (distance, points spent or
   affinity), and the cost is written on the node.
5. **Legibility comes from a small vocabulary and named places**, not from more labels: few node ranks with a colour each,
   a name per region and per board, the landmark visible at full zoom-out.
6. **Unequal is memorable.** Grim Dawn's unequal islands and PoE's ±15% sectors are what players remember a place by;
   Diablo's identical boards are a tool, not a place.

## What Bob must do that none of them had to

- **Carry the whole identity in the tree**: no ascendancy, mastery or skill tree takes the big decisions off it.
- **Serve a run that is mostly short**: the first twenty points must be a complete, satisfying tree on their own, and
  the tree must stay readable when opened for one point at a time.
- **Be honest about the auto-battler**: a node's cost or condition must be one the sheet shows and the sim produces.
- **Work on a phone** without hover: names on the picture, the card on tap, search, and one obvious next node per visit.
