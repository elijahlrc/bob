# D. The Strider section, rebuilt from the player's seat

> Written before the user's edit of this section (2026-10-19, folded into the files the same day). The walks and step

Second attempt at the Bow (2026-10-19), after the user's review of the first draft: a nonsense start, no highways,
fake micro-choices, long paths to nothing. The layout is new from scratch; the line vocabulary is kept. The listing is
`D-strider.md`, the pictures `D-strider.png` and `D-strider-text.png`.

## What was read first

**The reference's graph**, its dexterity class's start traced node by node from `reference/tree/ref-tree.json` (the reference tree: lines,
groups, links; no coordinates). Earlier phases only measured rates from that file and never read a start as a walk:

- The start touches **two +10 dexterity nodes**. Each is one step from a **bare dexterity hub of degree five to
  seven**. So point one is a side, and point two is a crossroads.
- The first notable (its attack-speed charges) sits on a **loop between the two hubs**; the other early notables (evasion
  with a charge; damage with every weapon) are about **attacks**, not bows. Nothing within three steps names a weapon.
- Off the hubs: **three-node pockets** (accuracy and attack speed, crit, evasion and resistances, life), each a dead
  end; the bow road; the centre. Its dodge keystone is **five steps** from the start, one node off a hub; its projectile-evasion keystone six.
- The skeleton is a **mesh of bare dexterity nodes** (degree 4 to 8) with pockets hanging off them; within ten steps,
  roughly a quarter of the nodes are bare attribute and almost every junction is one.

**The reference's layout**, looked at on its site (the live tree, the current version, the dexterity class's start) after the user asked why it had
never been looked at. The extraction had dropped coordinates under the no-copying rule and "never copy" had been read
as "never look". What it shows, and what the first version of this section got wrong:

- **Clusters are compact orbits.** The nodes of a cluster sit about a third of the distance between clusters apart;
  a pocket is a tight triangle or ring beside its junction, not three nodes in a line at road spacing.
- **Roads are long straight runs** that turn at a junction node, so the region reads as a few big rounded loops with
  the empty space inside them, not as a uniform web.
- **The big clusters are closed rings** with the notables on the rim, entered at one node and walkable through.
- **Density near the start is several times** what the first version had at the same extent. (On the live tree the
  cluster beside the start has an inert centre, a mastery added after the reference version, with its notable on the rim; the start's own
  nodes are no longer bare dexterity. The reference graph above is the one this section's structure follows.)

## Three walks, point by point

**A bow Strider (a physical bow gem at point five).** 1 north hub. 2 the bows road's first node. 3 its second. 4 bow
damage. 5 bow damage and life. 6 **Hunter's Heart** (bow damage, life, accuracy): the first bow notable, six points.
7 the road's third node. 8 the bows hub. 9 the wheel's entrance. 10 bow damage and speed. 11 the same. 12 **Longbow**
(the speed side; the crit side was the other choice at point 10). 13 back at the north hub: attack speed. 14 **Hawk's
Eye**. 15 the string's first node. 16 accuracy and attack speed. 17 **True Aim**, which also closes the loop onto the
bows road's second node. 18 the fourth bows junction. 19 bow damage. 20 **Steady Draw**. Every point from the second is
a choice between at least two things of different kinds, and nothing taken is wasted on this build.

**A thrown-steel or sword Strider (a sword and a thrown projectile gem).** 1 south hub. 2 the physical road's first
node. 3 its second. 4 its third. 5 the corner. 6 the physical row's first junction (six bare dexterity nodes: the price
of going straight to the weapon, and the gear wants them; Wayfarer off the second node and Hard Target off the third are
the stops along the way). 7 the wheel's entrance. 8 physical attack damage. 9 with life. 10 **Heavy Draw** (strength for
the sword). 11 the far node. 12 **Iron Tips**, the other side taken from the far end. 13 the bleed road's junction.
14 the bleed hub (the bleed wheel's entrance is the other choice here). 15 and 16 the two road nodes up to the
projectiles hub. 17 the hub. 18 the wheel's entrance. 19 projectile damage. 20 **Split Volley**. From here the string
leads home for 6 points, closing the loop, with Fleet Foot and Quick Hands beside it.

**A Reaver coming for the Bow's defences.** By the ring: the Reaver's chain and junction, four ring nodes, the Bow's
south hub (8 points, all attributes the Reaver's gear wants anyway), then evasion and **Hawk's Eye** at 10, **Long
Breath** at 11, **Wayfarer** at 13. By the near crossing from the Yard's dual-wield junction: one node, the physical
road's third junction, then **Hard Target** at 14 up the chain, or the corner and the physical row. Up the dear road from
the Yard's blood: **Plated Hide** at 16, one node off that road's second junction, so the five-node corridor has a
choice point in it. The ways in reach different things, so which one a Reaver takes depends on what it came for.
