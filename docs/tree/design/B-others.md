# B — The secondary references: Wolcen, Borderlands, Torchlight, Titan Quest and the reference game's sequel

Phase B of [PASSIVE-TREE.md](../../PASSIVE-TREE.md), section 2. Written 2026-10-19 from the web only. Every number is
marked **sourced (url)** or **recalled**; where sources disagree both are given. Fandom, the sequel's wiki, Maxroll and IGN were

## 1. Wolcen: Lords of Mayhem — the Gate of Fates

A classless ring tree; rotation stands in for highways.

**Shape and count.** Three concentric rings in **21 branches**: 3 inner, 6 middle, 12 outer; sourced
(https://wolcen.wiki.fextralife.com/Passive+Skills; "21 sections" in the studio's gameplay sheet,
https://wolcengame.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/WolcenGameplay.pdf). A fan count gives **492 nodes**: branches of 24
(inner), 30 (middle), 20 (outer), each in three sub-groups of **1 big + 4 to 11 small**, so about **63 big nodes**;
sourced (https://steamah.com/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem-gate-of-fates-guide/, unofficial). The wiki instead lists 2 to 3
"Major" nodes per branch; the counts are not reconciled, and neither says where in a branch the big node sits.

**Starting areas.** The centre touches the three inner branches, one per lineage (warrior, rogue, mage). The devs' 2016
diary: inner sections are "broader and well known fantasy archetypes", outer ones "more specialized and advanced";
sourced (https://www.gamebanshee.com/k9ahq). Every build starts from the same three choices.

**Highways: none; rotation instead.** Each ring turns, so any middle branch can sit under any inner branch and any outer
under any middle; a branch cut off from the centre keeps its points but "you will not gain the benefits until it
re-connects" (sourced, wiki above). Every allocated node is a stat node; reaching a far theme costs a rotation, not
points. Players: rotation "saves you passive points, because you don't have to spend them on random nodes in order to
reach across the tree" and "allows you to hybrid fairly well"; sourced
(https://steamcommunity.com/app/424370/discussions/4/1737760452994459087).

**Keystone-equivalents.** The big nodes are mostly buffs; the trades cluster in the outer ring. Wiki examples (sourced):
+100% crit damage with -30% damage; extra projectiles with -50% projectile damage; +2 ailment stacks with -30% non-DoT
damage; health capped at 5% of force shield; -20% health and shield for summons taking 25% less. About **8 of ~30 outer
majors** carry a penalty. The shape is the reference game's: a restriction paid for a multiplier.

**Decisions and budget.** One point per level to cap 90, so about **90 points**; sourced (gameplay sheet: "At each
level, players receive one point"; "up to level 90"); up to 40 more from endgame Operations, capped; sourced
(https://mp1st.com/title-updates-and-patches/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem-update-1-10-out-for-patch-8-this-may-22/). A build
meets: which inner branch (1), which middle and outer branches to rotate under it (2 to 4), and the rotation, which
players do **once per respec**: "you never needed to actually use it. You'd always just keep it in one configuration";
sourced (a thread on the reference game's official forum, 3067222). Respec costs a currency item (steamah).

**Communication and legibility.** Three colours run through all rings and "same color shares the same shape within each
ring" (sourced, steamah): lineage reads by colour and shape at any zoom. Reviews split: "labyrinthine"
(https://www.pcgamer.com/au/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem-review); "can be difficult to figure out"
(https://wolcen.wiki.fextralife.com/New+Player+Help); "a rotating puzzle you solve yourself"
(https://gamegeeker.com/games/wolcen-lords-of-mayhem). The devs' claim that rotation makes players "instinctively
understand the layout" (Kickstarter diary, search snippet) is not what the threads show: rotation is a setup step, not a map.

**Distinctive:** zero travel, one mechanism that makes any three themes adjacent. The price is that neighbourhood carries
no theme, since any branch may end up beside any other; colour does the work instead.

## 2. Borderlands 2 / 3 (and 4, for the rule)

Three narrow trees per class, gated by points spent, a capstone at the bottom of each. No travel.

**Structure.** **3 trees** per character, the action skill at the top, tiers downward; BL3 has tiers 1 to 6 with 3 to 5
skills per tier and tier 6 a single capstone; sourced
(https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/534383-borderlands-3-character-skill-trees-all-revealed-abilities). Most skills
take 5 ranks, capstones and augments 1; recalled.

**The gate.** A tier opens at **5 points** spent in the tiers above it, in that tree; for BL2, to reach tier 3 "you'd
first need to use ten points, split as at least five points between [two tier-1 skills] and at least five points between
[two tier-2 skills]"; sourced (https://steamcommunity.com/app/49520/discussions/0/1621726179571793018). The capstone
opens at 25 spent and is the **26th** point: "maxing out one skill tree takes 26 skill points"; sourced for BL3
(https://progameguides.com/borderlands/what-is-the-max-level-in-borderlands-3/); fan calculators give 25 or 26 for BL2
depending on whether the capstone point is counted.

**Budget.** BL3: **70 points** at level 72, **130 to fill** the three trees, so two capstones and never three; Designer's
Cut adds a fourth tree; sourced (https://mentalmars.com/game-news/what-is-the-max-level-cap-in-borderlands-3/). BL2: 76
at level 80, 88 with the Lilith DLC; sourced (https://west-games.com/krieg-skill-tree-calculator/,
https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/borderlands-2-can-you-max). A full build is **54%** of a character's tree.

**Decisions.** Two kinds: _which tree to commit to_ (26/26/18 or 26/44), and _which skills to fill inside a tier_ (3 to
5 offered, 5 points needed, so one or two taken). About 1 to 2 commitment decisions and about 12 tier decisions per full
build, all stat choices; no detour, no crossing. "Commit to a tree" comes entirely from the gate: a capstone needs 25
points in its own column and 70 cannot pay for three.

**Communication.** Three named, coloured columns; the row gate is printed on the row; the capstone is the bottom cell.
BL4's official page states the rule: "invest five of the Skill Points ... into a given row to unlock access to the next
row"; "A Capstone can be found at the bottom of each branch"; augments and capstones "must be equipped" and "Only one
Capstone ... equippable at a time"; sourced (https://borderlands.2k.com/borderlands-4/game-info/vault-hunters/vex/).
The whole plan reads on one screen: theme is a column, depth is commitment, the prize is at the bottom.

**Distinctive:** the gate by points-spent gives the walk-to-the-keystone feeling with zero travel nodes, and the column
silhouette explains itself. Themes cannot mix at the small-node level; a hybrid is 26 + 26 of two columns, never a blend.

## 3. Torchlight 2 and Torchlight: Infinite

**Torchlight 2.** **3 trees per class, 7 active + 3 passive skills each**; **15 ranks** per skill with **tier bonuses
at ranks 5, 10, 15** at no extra cost; a skill unlocks by character level, not by points in earlier skills; 1 point per
level plus Fame; only the last three points respec, for gold; sourced (https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-32255/,
https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/5996/Torchlight-II/). The lesson is the rank ladder: depth inside one node, with
thresholds that make "5 more here" a decision against "5 there". No travel, no clusters; the capstone is rank 15.

**Torchlight: Infinite (mobile-first).** **24 talent boards across 6 gods, 3 in use**: a base board at level 2, a
sub-board of that god at level 20, a third from any god; **1 of 3 Major talents** at **16 and 32 points** spent in a
sub-board, changeable free; minor talents unlock medium talents; **116 points** maximum; sourced (Maxroll via search
snippet, https://maxroll.gg/torchlight-infinite/getting-started/talent-trees). A 2022 guide gives 10/20 and 18/36 with
micro-talent tiers in steps of 3 (https://www.millenium.org/guide/395929.html), so the gates are tuned between seasons.
Each board's first pick is "one of the first 6 talents" of the god's tree and the recommended pick is marked with an
asterisk; resets free to level 80; sourced
(https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/torchlight-infinite/ti-talents-guide-en.html). "Check the in-game overview
to see what each tree specializes in"; sourced (https://www.pockettactics.com/torchlight-infinite/build).

**Hero traits.** Up to 3 per hero, with milestones at **levels 15, 32, 50, 62, 80**; sourced
(https://www.ginx.tv/en/torchlight-infinite-classes-and-hero-traits); guides disagree on whether a trait is permanent.
A trait is a class rule (ammo and reload, rage, fusion), not a stat: the keystone lives on the hero. Pactspirits are a
separate gacha board whose spirits "modify the effect of the Pact Points in their radius"; sourced
(https://sportskeeda.com/mmo/torchlight-infinite-best-pactspirits-use-category).

**Phone adaptation.** No fetched source describes layout or zoom. **Recalled:** each board is a compact lattice of small
square nodes around one core node, fits one phone screen without pan or zoom, is tapped in order along the lattice, and
the three boards are tabs. The sourced facts agree in direction: themes are labelled by the god's name and an overview,
the first node is a 1-of-6 pick, the board gates by points spent, not position.

**Distinctive:** three screen-sized boards, each with its own gate and its own 1-of-3 majors, in place of one big tree.
The decision count is explicit (3 boards x 2 majors x 3 options), the label is the board name, and nothing is larger
than a screen.

## 4. Titan Quest and the reference game's sequel

**Titan Quest.** Pick 2 masteries (8 in the base game, 11 with all expansions; sourced, TQ2 wiki below); 3 skill
points per level; each mastery's bar gives attributes and health per
point and unlocks tiers at **1, 4, 10, 16, 24, 32** (40 with Atlantis); skills in tier rows with synergies; sourced
(https://titanquest.fandom.com/wiki/Mastery, search snippet). TQ2 keeps the shape with a separate Divinity currency and
draws passives as circles, actives as squares; sourced (https://titanquest2.wiki.fextralife.com/Masteries). Distinctive:
the gate is itself useful (bar points give stats), so a deep tier is never wasted travel, and the steps grow (3, 6, 6,
8, 8) so each deeper tier costs more commitment.

**The sequel.** Node types: **attribute (travel) nodes giving +5 to an attribute the player chooses**, small, notable,
keystone, jewel socket; **no masteries**; **no life nodes**; sourced
(a vulkk.com passive tree guide, February 2025, and a Maxroll guide to the sequel's tree and dual specialisation). The attribute highways "create three
distinct rings around the Passive Tree" and "become more cost-efficient per point" farther out (vulkk). Six start positions
shared by class pairs (12 planned, 8 playable at 0.4; recalled), with class-specific start nodes. Size: "over 1,500 nodes" and **122 points** (player post,
the sequel's official forum, thread 3767066, April 2025) against **1,800+ nodes, 500+ notables, 18 keystones**
at 0.2.0, ~120 allocated (a switchbladegaming.com passive tree guide); neither is official.
Weapon-set points: a separate pool of **24**, two per specialisation book (a quest reward), spendable only with regular points in
hand, so one build can hold two trees; sourced (Maxroll above, a Mobalytics dual specialisation guide).
Respec costs gold, dynamic, attribute nodes half price (Maxroll).

**What the developer changed.** Official 0.3.0 notes: **105 new clusters** (15 to 25 per start, 8 between the strength
and intelligence starts); one small attribute node "removed on all of the outward
highways between class starting locations"; its evasion-to-dodge keystone removed; keystones sharpened into trades (its two-handed
keystone now halves the life granted by strength; its energy shield recharge keystone makes flasks recover 50% less); four new keystones; stated aim "build
diversity"; sourced (official forum, thread 3826682). 0.4.0: "more than 250 new Passive Skills" for the
side of a new class; sourced (official forum, thread 3883495). Reception: from a tree that "almost
feels empty" to one players found "overwhelming at first"; sourced
(a fan site's before-and-after article on the tree). The no-life-nodes
reasoning as relayed from the developer's 2023 convention, "its not fun to simply pick up %life nodes", is secondary only
(official forum, thread 3408808). **No primary source was found for why attribute nodes became a
choice or why masteries were dropped.** Choice-nodes returned in 0.5.4's endgame passive tree: "a choice between several
possible modifiers that you can flip between at will", 24 points, half notables; sourced
(PCGamesN on the 0.5.4 patch notes).

**Communication.** The game director: hovering a passive "should tell you what the DPS change will be on all of your
skills"; an imported build file "will highlight the nodes on your tree"; no in-game build planner because "that's the game telling
you that you're supposed to use it"; sourced (PCGamesN on the sequel's tooltips). Layout by attribute
(Intelligence top, Dexterity right, Strength lower-left, hybrids at junctions); hovering a distant notable previews the
cheapest path and its node count (switchblade guide). Player critique (forum 3767066): "Half the nodes seem like filler",
the +5 node is "demoralizing", and "travel nodes taking too much of points and too long travel distance".

**Distinctive:** the only secondary game with real travel, and its history is a tuning of it: the travel node was made
useful (a chosen attribute), then trimmed (one per highway), while supply widened (105 clusters, 250 nodes) and
keystones became trades. Players still name travel as the cost they resent.

## 6. What the secondary games suggest for Bob (suggestions, not decisions)

1. _Suggestion:_ every game but the reference game replaces travel with a gate by points spent (5 per tier, 16/32 per board, a mastery
   bar), and players of the one game with travel name it as the cost they resent. Bob could keep highways short and make
   each travel node give something a build wants (the sequel's chosen attribute), so the audit's "share of travel" can sit
   under the reference game's.
2. _Suggestion:_ "commit to a theme" needs no distance. Borderlands makes it with a capstone that needs 25 of its own
   column; Torchlight Infinite with a major at 16/32 points in its board. A Bob keystone that opens when N points sit in
   its region gives the same feeling with no road.
3. _Suggestion:_ keystones that are trades are the minority everywhere (about a quarter of Wolcen's outer majors; the sequel
   redesigned its own in 0.3.0). The brief should fix the share of keystones that are trades on purpose, written as a
   restriction paid for a multiplier, the shape all four games use.
4. _Suggestion:_ for a phone, Torchlight Infinite's answer is boards no larger than a screen with the theme as the tab
   name; Borderlands' is a column whose depth is commitment. Each Bob region could be legible whole at one zoom level,
   with its name as the label, before any node text is read.
5. _Suggestion:_ the sequel's 0.3.0 widening (105 clusters) is the one data point on how much supply a shared tree needs, and
   the reaction was "overwhelming". Bob's count should come from supply arithmetic (plan C9) and be checked against the
   time a player has between maps, not against the reference game's size.
6. _Suggestion:_ Wolcen's rotation warns against clever navigation: a mechanism used once and then ignored is not a
   decision. Anything set once per run in Bob (a start, a first keystone) should be designed as a commitment, not a tool.
