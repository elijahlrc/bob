# Bob — Audit of the item bases against PoE 3.9

Status: **audit done 2026-10-18; the fix pass followed the same day (next section).** It follows
[AUDIT-WEAPON-AFFIXES.md](AUDIT-WEAPON-AFFIXES.md) and [AUDIT-ARMOUR-AFFIXES.md](AUDIT-ARMOUR-AFFIXES.md) and covers every base an
item can be of: weapons, armour pieces, shields, rings, amulets, belts, quivers and flasks. The sections after the fix pass describe the
game as it was on the day of the audit. Reference text and numbers live only in this file and under `docs/coverage/` (DESIGN §3);
nothing here goes in `src/`.

## The fix pass (2026-10-18)

The author asked for every base of the reference game, not only the numbers of the bases Bob had. What was built:

- **One Bob base for every droppable 3.9 base: 774 of them, from 273.** Each carries the reference base's level, attribute
  requirements, defences or weapon numbers, block, flask amounts and charges, and implicit (a rolled range at its middle). The rows
  are `src/data/baseTables.ts`, generated by `npm run coverage:emit-bases` (`scripts/coverage/emit-bases.ts`) from
  `docs/coverage/pob-bases.json` and **`docs/coverage/base-names.json`**, which gives every reference base our own name and is the
  join a reference unique uses to find its base (`scripts/coverage/bobBase.ts`); `src/data/bases.ts` and `src/data/flasks.ts` turn
  the rows into bases. `npm run coverage:bases-audit` compares the two sides and prints what differs (nothing, at parity).
- **Ids are keyed by level** (`body_ar_l59`, `sword_l60`, `flask_life_l42`; a second base at a level takes a letter). The ids the
  game had before (`body_ar_1`–`4`, `sword_1`–`5`, `flask_life_1`–`8`, ...) are aliases of the base of their family nearest their old
  level, resolved inside `itemBase()` and `flaskBase()`, so saved items, posted rivals and hand-written uniques keep working; a new
  item carries the base's own id. The jewellery kinds keep their ids.
- **Every base's numbers are the reference's, weapons included.** A weapon's base damage, attacks per second, critical strike
  chance and reach are now its own, not its class's: the tiers of ×1.9 and the one-hand bonus of ×1.6 are gone. A top one-handed
  weapon does about 0.45 of what it did, a bow 0.36, a two-hander 0.5, a wand 0.67 (section 3); `WEAPON_DAMAGE_SCALE` in `bases.ts`
  (1) is the lever if that proves wrong. This supersedes the weapon audit's decision 5 of 2026-10-16, which had left base damage
  alone. Requirements are the reference's too (`REQUIREMENT_SCALE`, 1): a focused character meets its own class's (section 7),
  hybrid and off-class gear now asks for attributes the tree has to give.
- **Flasks** are the twelve life, twelve mana and six hybrid tiers with the reference amounts, seconds and charges, and all fifteen
  utility kinds. New: the Prism Draught (all resistances), Mire Draught (chaos resistance), Onrush Draught (Quickened while it runs),
  Keen Draught (critical strike chance is lucky), Ghoststep Draught (dodge; phasing is not modelled), Smokeveil Draught (evasion and a
  smoke cloud), Chillwake Draught (chilled ground and a chance to avoid cold damage from hits), Sanctum Draught (damage and
  consecrated ground) and Stonehide Draught (physical damage reduction and reflect). The engine gained a flask that leaves ground
  (`FlaskBase.ground`, `useFlask`), a flask that holds a buff (`grants`), a lucky critical strike chance (`critLucky`:
  `1 − (1 − c)²` on the sheet, the sim takes the sheet's number) and a chance to take none of a hit's damage of a type
  (`avoidHit.<type>`, `Defence.avoidHit`). The Bulwark and Mist Draughts give +3000 at level 27 and no longer scale with item level.
- **Jewellery** gained the kinds Bob lacked: the Gilt Ring, Coin Charm and Gilt Sash (item rarity, which drops read), the Hollow Ring
  and Gemmed Quiver (a socket of their own: `ItemBase.sockets`, `socketLimit`), and the levels and values of the rest are the
  reference's (a ring of fire resistance is level 16, not 5; +25 mana, not +20). Two bases with no reference source stay for the
  items on them and never drop: the Plated Girdle (`belt_armour`, three hand-written uniques) and the Feathered Quiver
  (`quiver_acc`, the Strider's first quiver).
- **Weapon families.** Rune daggers and warstaves are the 15 and 9 bases of the class split of 3.8 (the files carry them as daggers
  and staves; the split is this audit's, from the class lists), with their reference implicits (critical strike chance; block while
  wielding a staff). The Beckoning Wand (the one base that rolls minion affixes) carries `minionWeapon`, and five minion families roll
  on it with the reference tiers: minion damage, damage with mana, attack and cast speed (Bob's one minion speed stat), minion life
  and the minion gem level (`src/data/affixes.ts`). Minion resistances and accuracy have no stat and are left out.
- **Implicit armour bases.** The eleven of the reference are in: a plate of all resistances, a vestment of spell damage, a shroud of
  mana, a jerkin of movement speed, the three two-resistance boots, gloves of melee, projectile attack and spell damage, and a crown
  of minion damage.
- **Drops.** A drop is still the best base of its class the item level allows six times in ten; the other four now come from the
  bases within twenty levels below it (`BASE_DROP_WINDOW`), not the whole ladder, so a map-80 player does not find a level-6
  chestplate. A weapon drop picks one of the fifteen families (a thrusting sword, a rune dagger and a warstaff as readily as the
  class they sit in), where it picked one of the twelve classes and the class's best base decided the family.
- **Uniques** were re-emitted (`npm run coverage:emit`) onto their exact reference bases, and the unique translator no longer
  scales flat armour, evasion and energy shield lines down (it did by 0.6 and 0.45 to match the lighter bases): 104 flat lines rose
  with them. A hand-written unique whose level was below its base's drops at the base's level.
- **What an item is worth changed with its base.** A base's numbers are read when an item is used, not copied into it, so every
  item in a save, every posted rival and every duel replay has its base's new numbers.

Not reproduced, by decision (section 10): the hidden movement speed penalty of armour bodies and shields (the extractor never kept
it and no source was at hand), phasing, minion resistances and accuracy on the wand, the Demigod bases, Sacrificial Garb (a seventh
defence type), Keyblade, Charan's Sword, the Stygian Vise, the Breach Ring and the talismans. The flask affixes are the one item pool
still unaudited.

**Open.** The sheet's strongest-bleed model undercounts the bleed of a fast, low-damage bow (`npm run dpsinspect -- gougeShot
--weapon bow_l5`: the sheet 86, the sim 119 a second; a level-36 or higher bow agrees within 2%). The gem kit's weapons are now the
base of each family nearest level 45 (`src/sim/gemKit.ts`), where the fences hold; the model gap is a calc task of its own.

**Measured.** The weapon pass's harness: 16 bot runs per class to map 25 (`scripts/simpar.sh`), median map reached, in the
order vanguard, strider, mystic, reaver, zealot, shade. Before, seed 1: 20, 17, 18, 17, 14.5, 20. After, seed 1: 13.5, 19, 20,
20, 20, 20; seed 2: 20, 19, 20, 18.5, 13.5, 20; seed 3: 19.5, 17, 15, 19.5, 10, 20. The spread between seeds is as wide as any
difference between before and after, so to map 25 there is no sign of a change beyond it; that run barely sees the weapon change
(the first three tiers were within 0.7 to 1.4 of the reference already), and the late game, where a top weapon now does half of
what it did, has no bot measurement (the bot dies at the gate bosses). The Mystic's map-1 check: 0 of 40 died before, 0 of 40
after. Two single-class probes of the levers at seed 1, where the Vanguard reached 13.5: requirements at 0.65 of the reference,
15; weapon damage at 1.3, 20. An easy-difficulty probe to map 60 (two runs per class) showed what the loot side does with real
requirements: a level-63 Vanguard with 291 strength still swinging a level-17 rapier, because a weapon drop is any of fifteen
families and a found weapon of the wrong kind now asks for an attribute the character lacks; weighting weapon kinds by the
class's attribute, as `gemWeight` does for gems, is the follow-up on that side.

## Scope, sources and method

**What counts.** Every `ItemBase` of `src/data/bases.ts` (weapons, armour, shields, jewellery) and every `FlaskBase` of
`src/data/flasks.ts`, with its level, requirements, numbers and implicit, and the rules that draw a base for a drop (`rollBase`,
`src/gen/loot.ts`).

**The reference side.** `docs/coverage/pob-bases.json`: the base tables of Path of Building v1.4.155 (tag of 2019-12-14, the last
release before 3.10), 1,038 rows, of which 774 are droppable bases of the classes Bob has (the rest: jewels, talismans, the quest,
league and Demigod bases of section 9). A base's level, strength, dexterity and intelligence, armour, evasion, energy shield, block,
physical damage, critical strike chance, attacks per second, range, flask life, mana, seconds and charges, and implicit text. The
files carry no class split for rune daggers and warstaves and no movement speed penalty.

**Method.** A script put each Bob base beside the reference base of its class and type at the highest level a player of the Bob
base's level could use, and beside the best of the type; and, over the run, the best base a level allows in each game at every
fifth level. The 3.9 implicit lines went through the unique translator (`scripts/coverage/modDict.ts`), which mapped all but four
(block recovery, dodging spell hits, flat damage to bow attacks, and a belt's armour with evasion); rules were added.

## Headline (the game on the day of the audit)

1. **Bob had 273 droppable bases where 3.9 has 774.** Four armour tiers per slot and type (levels 1, 20, 40, 60) against 8 to 17;
   five weapon tiers (1, 15, 30, 45, 60) against 20 to 26 per class; one block and one attack speed per class against a value per
   base; 23 flasks against 45. Section 1.
2. **Between tiers a player wore much less than a 3.9 player.** The best body armour a level allows was 0.31 to 0.39 of the
   reference's at levels 10 to 15 and 30 to 35, 0.5 to 0.6 at 25, 45 and 55, and 0.62 (armour) or 0.56 (evasion) from level 65 on,
   where Bob's ladder ended and the reference's went on to 70. Helmets, gloves, boots and shields the same. Section 2.
3. **Energy shield ran the other way.** Energy shield bases were 1.0 to 1.5 times the reference's on a body or helmet, 1.3 to 2.0
   on gloves and boots, and a spirit shield 1.4 to 2.2. Section 4.
4. **Weapon damage grew about twice as fast as the reference's.** Tier 1 and 2 were near the reference (0.7 to 1.3), tier 5 was 2.1
   to 2.9 times it for attack weapons (a bow 2.76, a dagger 2.91), 1.5 to 1.6 for a wand or staff, from the ×1.9 per tier and the
   one-hand bonus. Section 3.
5. **Requirements were 0.5 to 0.65 of the reference's**: a level-60 sword asked for 68 strength and 68 dexterity against 113 and
   113, a level-60 mace 104 against 212, a plate 104 against 160. Section 7.
6. **Flasks recovered 0.4 to 0.7 of the reference's**, over 4 seconds instead of 6 to 8, with charges fixed at 21/7 where the
   reference's grow with the tier; 6 utility kinds of 15. Section 6.
7. **Jewellery was close** (the armour pass had built a base for each kind): a handful of levels and values off, five kinds missing
   (rarity, a socket), two Bob-only kinds. Section 5.
8. **Implicits and the rest.** Eleven armour bases with an implicit had no Bob base; the rune dagger and warstaff implicits were
   Bob's own; the Convoking Wand's minion affixes had no base to roll on. Section 8.

## 7. Requirements

Bob's `attrReq(level)` was 8 + 1.6 × level for one attribute, 0.65 of that for each of two. The reference's grow faster and differ
by class and type: at level 60 a plate asks 160 strength, a helmet 138, gloves and boots about 100; a two-handed sword 113 strength
and 113 dexterity, a mace 212 strength, a wand 212 intelligence, a bow 212 dexterity; a hybrid body about 96 of each. Bob asked 104
(one attribute) or 68 (two). The bot's attributes (two runs per class at easy difficulty, reading the character at every tenth map):
the main attribute is 100 to 140 at level 8, 120 to 180 at 20, 150 to 215 at 35, 175 to 245 at 45, 215 to 305 at 55 and 207 to 418
at 63 to 66; the second attribute 14 to 130. So a character built for its own class meets the reference requirements of its own
kind of gear (a Vanguard at level 64 with 207 to 225 strength against 212 for the heaviest mace, 160 for a plate), and hybrid or
off-class gear now asks for attributes the tree has to supply, as it does in the reference game. Taken as the reference's;
`REQUIREMENT_SCALE` is the lever.

## 8. Implicits and special bases

- **Armour with an implicit.** The reference has eleven droppable armour bases with one (all resistances on Astral Plate, spell damage
  on Occultist's Vestment, mana on Carnal Armour, movement speed on Assassin's Garb, two resistances on the three Two-Toned Boots,
  melee damage on Spiked Gloves, projectile attack damage on Gripped Gloves, spell damage on Fingerless Silk Gloves, minion damage on
  Bone Helmet); Bob had none. All translate; all were added.
- **Sockets on jewellery.** Unset Ring and Ornate Quiver have a socket; the slot has none otherwise. Added as a base field.
- **The Convoking Wand** "can roll minion modifiers": 15 families in the reference tables under that tag (minion damage with 8 tiers
  from 10–19% to 75–79%, damage with mana with 7, attack and cast speed with 7, +1 to minion gems at level 60, minion life with 6,
  minion movement speed with 3, minion resistances with 4, minion accuracy with 5). The weapon pass had dropped minion damage from
  weapons because this base did not exist.
- **Flask grounds.** Stibnite (a smoke cloud), Aquamarine (chilled ground) and Sulphur (consecrated ground) create ground on use;
  the engine's fields had the three kinds already (from Smoke Mine, Frostblink and the consecrated-ground skills), but no flask left
  one.

## 9. Left out of the reference side

Not droppable, or not a base in the game's sense: the five Demigod bases (Golden Wreath, Caligae, Bracers, Mantle, Flame), the
Sacrificial Garb (a three-way defence type Bob does not have), Keyblade and Charan's Sword (quest bases), the Stygian Vise and
Breach Ring (league mechanics), the 35 talisman amulets, and jewels. The uniques on them map to the nearest base of their class, as
before. The flask bases of unique flasks stay Bob's own (`uniqueOnly`): their effect is the unique's.

## 10. Decisions (2026-10-18)

1. **All bases, at the reference numbers**, weapons' damage included (the author's ask, superseding the weapon audit's decision 5).
   Recorded consequence: a top attack weapon does about half of what it did; the monster side (difficulty, life, the tree's pricing)
   was tuned against the old numbers; `WEAPON_DAMAGE_SCALE` is the lever and the bot measurement at the end of the fix pass is the
   first reading.
2. **Requirements at the reference numbers** (section 7); `REQUIREMENT_SCALE` is the lever.
3. **Level-keyed ids with aliases for the old ones**, never a renumbering: ids live in saves, posted rivals, uniques, the classes'
   starting gear and tests.
4. **Names are ours**, one per reference base, in `docs/coverage/base-names.json`; the armour ladders are an adjective per defence
   type with a noun per slot (as before), shields, weapons and flasks are listed. No name is a reference base name, and the IP scan
   (`src/data/ip.test.ts`) holds.
5. **Drops** draw the other 40% from the twenty levels below the best base, not the whole ladder.
6. **The Beckoning Wand's minion families** use Bob's tier odds (the top tier a quarter as likely as the lowest), as every family
   does, not the reference's 750-to-20 cliff; minion attack and cast speed map to Bob's one minion speed stat; minion resistances,
   accuracy and movement speed are left out (no stat, or the same stat twice).
7. **Not modelled:** the hidden movement speed penalty of armour bodies and shields (no number at hand; the extractor keeps none),
   phasing, the Demigod and quest bases.
8. **Two Bob-only bases stay and never drop** (Plated Girdle, Feathered Quiver): the items and uniques on them, and the Strider's
   first quiver, keep resolving.
9. **The gem kit wears level-45 bases** so its level-50 character is geared as before; the strongest-bleed gap of the sheet on a
   weak bow is recorded as open, not papered over.
10. **A wand keeps Bob's reach of 1.2 tiles.** The reference's 120 units is the reach of its projectile, which Bob's wand attack
    is not; converted like a melee reach it came to 14 tiles and had the Mystic standing off at that distance (caught before the
    measurement was written up).
11. **A weapon drop picks one of the fifteen families, not one of the twelve classes.** Picking the class and then its best base
    left the family with the later base (a dagger over a rune dagger, a sword over a thrusting sword) with almost every drop of a
    stretch of levels (rune daggers 0.6% of drops against daggers 1.9%); each family is now about 2% of drops, as the reference's
    separate classes are.
12. **`SAVE_VERSION` is not bumped.** The save format is unchanged, an old id still resolves, and a bump would empty the rival pool
    of every character posted so far; a tab on an older build that meets an id it cannot resolve drops that rival (`itemOk`).
