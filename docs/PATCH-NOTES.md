# Patch notes

Player-facing notes, newest first, shown on the Patch notes screen of the main menu. Add an entry for every change a player
would notice. Plain text: no markdown emphasis or code, no first person, no file names or internal terms, nothing that did not
change, and our own names only. Dash bullets under plain headings.

## 2026-10-28

- Crushing Blow hits about 17% harder.
- Refine Gem and Exalted Focus are removed; a saved run that holds either loses it.
- The Items tab opens a page for the picked item, with Compare beside what it would replace and Swap to wear it.
- Hooks that pull your character stop at the first wall, and a character left in rock or off the map is set back on the floor.
- Saves take about a quarter of the space, and when the browser has no room for a run a warning says so instead of the game crashing.
- The game now has interface sounds and music.

## 2026-10-26

### Skills

- Skill damage is rebalanced across the board: attacks no longer gain damage from your level and hit much less hard later on, while spells and most minions hit much harder and damage over time somewhat harder.
- Many skills are reworked to hit as intended: Spinning Dash hits each enemy only once a dash, Rain of Steel falls in five volleys instead of six, and Faultline's aftershock hits much harder.
- Mines can lie fifteen at a time and go off as soon as they land, and Shrapnel Trap bursts into smaller blasts instead of throwing extra traps.
- Exalted Focus raises skill levels much more slowly as you level, Double Set adds traps by chance, and Rime Herald's and Storm Herald's hits now grow with your spell damage.
- Call Furies builds a swarm of up to twenty spirits, Crushing Halo's hits ignore some armour, and Skitter Flash's sparks last twice as long.
- Glacier Dart now looks like a slow, heavy crystal and Chilling Shard like a thin sliver that fades as it flies.
- Call Furies, Pack of Hounds and Brood of Spiders can be set to Main, Periodic or Off on the Strategy tab, so they no longer take every turn beside a damage skill.
- The same support gem no longer stacks on a skill: linked twice, only the higher level works, and the spare says so.

### Monsters and maps

- Warden Pylons' protection now drops for one second in every six, and your character no longer wastes hits on monsters it covers.
- Map bonuses that promised currency now pay in items instead.

### Items and interface

- The kill counter now counts out of the monsters on the map, and an XP bar with your level runs across the top of the screen.
- The Character tab shows what each attribute gives, and every number's tip works out its formula with your own numbers.
- The Skills tab is reworked: tap a gem or socket, then a lit spot to place or swap it, and a skill's Supports button chooses all its supports in one place.
- The Upgrades list now suggests flasks that improve on one you wear.
- Several uniques now cast skills of their own, from a lightning bolt to spectral hounds, spiders and fire with each step.
- The Mystic's west starting passive gives flat energy shield, and the life orb's tooltip shows your energy shield.
- Fixed a black screen at map start on phones and a Skills search that could hide every gem, now with a Clear filters button; high scores scroll sideways on phones.
- Your character now fetches every drop, however far it fell, and a fight no longer makes it give up on one.
- The Strategy tab has a Damage details panel showing how often each skill is used and its damage by type, minions and clones included.
- The Patch notes screen shows a few releases at a time, with a button for older ones.
- Fixed the no-usable-skill warning showing when Call Furies, Pack of Hounds or Brood of Spiders is your only main skill.

## 2026-10-17

- Duels: when a run ends, you can fight the characters other players left behind, each with its own build, in a round arena. The button is on the end screen.
- Duelling needs a name: type one in the rivals box on the title or end screen. Your character is then sent for others to fight, and your IP address is kept hashed to block abuse.
- Ratings start at ten times a character's level. The first fight against each rival is rated, later ones are practice. High scores on the title screen list the top-rated characters. Ratings are new and may be reset.
- In a duel both sides take about a third of normal damage, and a fight ends on a death, after 90 seconds, or after 15 seconds without a hit.
- Bosses say a line when you first meet them. The Shrine and the Ferryman have one too.
- On iPhone and iPad the "Failed to start the audio device" crash screen is gone. Errors that do not stop the game now show a small message instead of the crash screen.
- The duel button now sits at the top of the end screen, and the rivals box is ticked from the start, so a name is all you need to type.

## 2026-10-16

- Weapons roll many more affixes. Every weapon can roll resistances, life and mana leech from physical attack damage, life and mana on kill, added chaos damage, a damage over time multiplier, physical damage with accuracy, increased accuracy and reduced attribute requirements. Spell weapons also roll fire, cold and lightning damage, spell critical strike chance, burning damage, damage over time multipliers by type, extra chaos damage and spell gem levels. Bows roll an additional arrow, bows and wands roll projectile speed, and melee weapons and bows roll levels for their own socketed gems.
- A weapon now rolls what suits its kind: wands only intelligence, bows dexterity, maces strength, swords and axes strength and dexterity, claws and daggers dexterity and intelligence. Claws and daggers can roll maximum mana. Ignite, freeze and shock chance are for wands, sceptres and staves; bleed and poison chance, now with increased damage with that ailment, are for the melee weapons and bows that use them. Area of effect no longer rolls on weapons. Any attack weapon can roll +1 to socketed gems; two-handed weapons no longer roll +2, and staves no longer roll it at all.
- Flat elemental attack damage on weapons is about three quarters larger at the top on one-handed weapons, more on two-handed weapons and bows; flat physical is larger below the top tiers, and at the top only on two-handed weapons and bows. Flat spell damage on spell weapons is larger, most on staves. Spell damage on one-handed spell weapons reaches 109% (was 89%), on staves 164%. Cast speed tops out at 25% (was 29%) on one-handed spell weapons, 38% on staves. Attack speed on bows and wands tops out at 19% (was 27%). Increased physical and elemental attack damage roll less often on weapons.
- The rune-carved daggers (Etched Bone Knife to Cairn-script Blade) now roll spell affixes, and the fighting staves (Brawler's Pole to Colossus Warstaff) no longer do. Weapon built-in bonuses are reworked: most swords give increased accuracy, rapiers and foils give critical strike multiplier, two-handed maces give stun duration, staves lose their spell damage and give block only, some bows gain critical strike multiplier or elemental attack damage, and claws, daggers, wands, sceptres and one-handed maces have new values.
- Flat accuracy on a weapon now counts for that weapon alone, and life gained per enemy hit from items works only for attacks, not for spells.
- Item cards show a weapon's damage, attack speed and critical strike chance after its affixes, with changed numbers in colour, and a line for added elemental and chaos damage. Comparing items now marks a stat whose number changed as changed, not as lost and gained. Ailment chances read as, for example, 5% chance to Ignite, and plain spell damage as increased Spell Damage.
- Affix tiers count down from the best: tier 1 is the strongest. On every item the best tier of an affix is now a quarter as likely as the lowest, where it was about half, so top rolls come less often.
- Armour, shields, rings, amulets, belts and quivers roll a fuller set of affixes, each suited to the item. Armour that gives two defences rolls only the paired defence affixes, never the single ones. Strength, dexterity and intelligence follow the defence type (helmets also roll intelligence, gloves dexterity), and maximum mana now rolls only on armour with energy shield, body armour and shields included.
- New armour and jewellery affixes: flat and percent defence pairs, defence with life or mana, defence with faster stun and block recovery, reflected physical damage, extra physical damage reduction, dodge, spell block, avoiding elemental ailments, regenerating life or energy shield by percent, life and mana on kill, mana leech, fire, cold and lightning damage on rings and amulets, melee and minion gem levels, spell affixes on energy shield shields, projectile speed and damage over time on quivers, and flask affixes on belts.
- Numbers now scale with the slot. Flat armour tops out at 400 on body armour and 60 on gloves and boots; life at 129 on body armour and 79 on a ring. Amulet spell damage tops out at 26% (was 89%) and cast speed at 13% (was 29%). Life per hit on gloves and rings tops out at 2 and on amulets at 4 (was 10). Leech on gloves, rings and quivers tops out at 0.4% (was 1%), all leech on armour and jewellery counts physical attack damage only, and it starts at item level 50. Movement speed is now a boots prefix that reaches 35%.
- Levels to all socketed gems on helmets and body armour, ignite, bleed, poison, shock and freeze chance on gloves, added chaos damage to attacks, area of effect, aura effect and reduced mana reserved no longer roll on armour or jewellery.
- Shields come in six types: the three pure ones and armour with evasion, armour with energy shield, and evasion with energy shield. Every type has a built-in bonus: life, movement speed, spell damage, block recovery, elemental resistance or dodge. Rings, amulets, belts and quivers have 55 bases, up from 14, each with its own bonus, and any base your item level allows is as likely as another.
- Blocking now staggers your character for about a third of a second, as a stun does. Faster stun and block recovery shortens it, and stuns on you as well. Block builds are somewhat weaker. New affixes: chance to block projectile attacks, and life or mana gained when you block.
- Armour cards show a piece's defences and block after its affixes, with changed numbers in colour.
- Items you already have keep their affixes and built-in bonuses; the changes apply to items found from now on.
- Uniques whose life or mana leech rolls between two values with decimals now roll within that range. A fault had made them roll either the lowest value or one point above it, so Last Sprint could have 1.6% life leech instead of 0.6–0.8%. This affected Last Sprint, Slaughter Pendant, Drake Hide Plate, Ember Halo Mail, Many-Eyed Circlet, Hollow Regent Mask, Wraith Razor and Marksman's Pendant. Copies you already own keep their rolls.
- A new run starts straight in its first map.
- After a map that dropped something or offers a reward, the camp opens on your items, showing what the map dropped, with any reward to pick at the top. A Next button at the bottom right steps through Items, Skills, the passive tree when you have points to spend, and the choice of the next map, so you can review everything by clicking in one place.
- The Skills tab shows how many new gems you have.
- Auto-continue is gone: you choose every map.
- Map cards are shorter. The "Asks" line, the tags, the Gentle, Even and Fierce label, the DPS and effective life line and the Comfortable, Close and Dangerous verdict are gone, and the rewards table takes two columns.
- Each class on the class screen says how it fights.
- When a run ends, you can start a new run of the same class straight away.
- Swapping gear says what happens to your gems: a row of sockets before and after, and a line such as "No socket for Twin Cut, it will be unequipped" when a gem loses its place.
- On a phone, selecting an item lists every stat that changes against what you wear, with the old values. One tap opens the full card, and Both adds the two cards and the changes to damage, effective HP, life and resistances.
- Worn gear shows its sockets and names its gems, with a link to edit them on the Skills tab. Bag rows show their sockets and the item's first stats, and warn when a swap would send gems to the bag. On a phone the bag filters sit behind a Filter button, with Clear filters beside it.
- Putting a gem from the bag into a filled socket names the gem it replaces and asks first, on the Skills tab and now on the Items tab, where a bag gem can go in any socket you choose, each showing what it gains. The Skills tab says which slot a gem helps most and why a support would do nothing there.

## 2026-10-14

- Skills that set something down now show it. Traps and mines are thrown in an arc and a closing ring shows them arming, totems and brands drop into place, and lasting ground, pods, ice crystals, storms, orbs, trails, illusions, geysers and smoke stay visible while they last. Glacier Wall raises blocks of ice, and the burst as it goes up now hurts enemies along the whole wall instead of a circle at its middle.
- The area a skill lands now shows what the skill is inside a fine outline: slam cracks, spikes, frost crystals, flames, lava, lightning bolts, plague cloud, holy light, shrapnel, jaws and blades.
- Sets of traps, mines and totems, such as Double Set, Triple Snare, Snare Cluster, Field of Charges and Totem Choir, no longer burst all on one spot: their areas fan out round the target, so they cover more of a pack. Repeated areas, Mortar Bow's arrows and Slag Blow's balls also land apart from each other.
- Whirring Motes now shock nearby enemies by the amount their card says. A fault had made those enemies take many times normal damage, so builds that use the motes will deal much less.
- The damage figures on the Character sheet and the map cards are closer to what the game deals. They now follow the skills your character actually uses in turn, and count the extra blasts, bolts, projectiles, repeats and standing traps and mines that skills add.
- Support gem cards now list the skills they will not take, such as Triple Cadence and channelled skills, so "Supports any skill" is no longer misleading.
- Livewire Snares and Livewire Charges are renamed Static Snares and Static Mines.
- The stop screens, Respite and map-type cards, and the boss map card now use fewer words: they say what you get and what you choose, and no longer list what the level does not give.
- New enemy affix, Blinking: the monster marks the spot where you stand and, a moment later, teleports onto it with a travel spell of its element. Cinder Step bursts in fire and leaves burning ground, Rime Step bursts in cold and leaves chilling ground, Bolt Step flashes at both ends, and a plain one just lands hard. Stun it while it gathers to stop it.
- Wisps, Lantern Wights and Bone Crawlers can now teleport onto you from a distance too, after a warning on the ground, with Bolt Step, Rime Step and Quick Step.
- The Siege Marshal keeps calling regiments for as long as the fight lasts, the same three over again, instead of coming down alone after the third. Each time you clear a regiment he comes down for six seconds, fights you himself and takes extra damage, and then the horn sounds for the next. If a regiment is still standing 22 seconds after it came, the next arrives anyway, but no more come while ten soldiers stand.
- The early game is much easier. Enemies still have half the life and deal half the damage on map 1, but they now climb back to the usual strength gradually, reaching it on map 20 instead of map 5. Bosses and mini-bosses on the way are softer too: the Tollkeeper on map 10 has about three quarters of its old life and hits about a quarter softer. Your character is unchanged.
- Enemies that slam, lob or burst the ground now do it half as often and twice as hard, with the same warning, so a pack leaves fewer overlapping warnings and pools. Slag Brutes leave fewer, larger pools of fire behind them.
- Your character now dodges for at most 10 seconds at a time, then stands its ground and fights for 5 seconds, so a crowded fight can no longer keep it dodging for ever.
- New stops. From map 6, one level in every five offers a stop in place of one of its maps. Taking one spends the level, with no experience, loot or reward pick, and gives a service a map cannot. You can always take a map instead. There are five: the Awlsmith, the Gem Hoard, the Shrine, the Quartermaster and, from map 8, the Ferryman.
- The Awlsmith bores one socket into an item of your choice for free. More sockets on the same item in that visit cost Bone Dust: nothing up to its third socket, 40 for the fourth, 120 for the fifth and 300 for the sixth. The Gem Hoard lets you name one of four kinds of gem, then take 3 of the 8 laid out.
- The Shrine lets you choose one of three blessings, such as 22% more maximum life, 15% more damage, +26% to all resistances or +18% movement speed. It lasts until your next stop and replaces the blessing you had.
- The Quartermaster sells eight wares for Bone Dust: two good rare items, a gem, a utility flask, three provisions and a reprieve. Whatever you leave is gone. The Ferryman gives you the reprieve of your choice for nothing if your pouch has room, but you carry a curse of 15% less maximum life for the next five levels.
- New provisions. Your pouch (the Satchel in camp) holds three. Arm any or all of them before a map; they help for that map only and are used up when it starts. Whetted Powder, Ward-oil and Bloodsalt are small and safe. Glass Draught, Greedy Lantern, Rushing Tonic and Hunter's Bell are strong and cost something. A cleared map from map 3 may leave one in your pouch, and reward picks may offer one.
- New reprieves, found from map 5 in reward picks, at the Quartermaster and from the Ferryman. They sit in your pouch, need no arming, fire by themselves when you would die and refill all your flasks. Ferry Token pulls you out of the map with what you picked up, but does nothing on a boss level. Last Spark brings you back at 35% life, keeps you from harm for 3 seconds and throws nearby enemies back. Bargain Coin brings you back at 50% life, but you carry the Ferryman's curse for five levels.
- Currency no longer drops: no crafting currencies, essences, tablets or Wayfinder's Chalk from enemies, champions or reward picks, and the Quartermaster sells none. Bone Dust from salvaging items is now the only money, and what you already hold is kept.

## 2026-10-13

- The first four maps are much easier: enemies have half the life and deal half the damage on map 1, climbing back to the usual strength by map 5. Your character is unchanged.
- New Last map tab in camp, and the same report on the death screen: for the map just played, the damage each skill, minion and ailment dealt by type, and what hurt you, by source and type.
- Enemies with energy shield now show it on their health bar, a blue strip above the life that drains first. The enemy card shows it too.
- Drain Trap now draws its beams, a glowing line from each enemy it holds to you with a red and a blue mote running along it for the life and mana it gives.
- Skills that leave something standing are now drawn: the blades of Orbiting Blades, the Tempest Mote orb, Skyfall's markers, Fuse Arrow's arrows, Blight Hail's pods, the Twin Shadow archer, Cinder Blow's charges and Slag Carapace's shell.
- Every buff that is up now shows on your character in its own colour and style: speed buffs leave streaks behind you, guards are plates or a halo, surges of power circle your body, rages give off embers, banners fly a small flag, stances ring your feet, and so on. Buffs of the same kind have different colours, and several at once share one place on the body (three guards make one ring of plates in three colours) instead of drawing over each other. A buff about to end blinks.
- Skills that deal no hit of their own, such as Drain Trap, Creeping Plague and Withering Breath, now give what a hit gives (charges, buffs, rage, life and mana) when they land, so Static Snares works with Drain Trap. Curses do not.
- Every map now has a biome: Crypt, Cavern, Ruins, Marsh, Forge, Hollow, Shrine or Warren. It sets the layout and look of the map and is shown when you enter. It is separate from the theme, so any theme can turn up in any biome.
- New layouts: Caverns and Marshes are winding caves, the Hollow is wide open ground with pillars, Ruins have more than one way round, and the Warren is a tangle of small chambers and narrow passages.
- Each biome has its own floors, walls, lighting and decorations, such as pools, moss, glowing fungus, lava cracks, candles, roots and rubble.
- Maps now grow steadily with depth, some a little bigger or smaller than others at the same level, and packs follow the size of their room, so deeper maps hold somewhat more monsters.
- From map 3 a map can be Sparse, Packed, Clustered or Scattered, shown on its offer. Sparse has fewer monsters but more magic and rare ones, Packed half as many again, Clustered a few large packs, and Scattered small groups everywhere.
- Quarry is always set in a Cavern, Holdout in a Hollow, Collapse in Ruins and Crawl in a Warren.
- The new biomes are still being balance-tested. For now Caverns and Marshes are harder than the others and the Hollow and the Warren a little easier, so runs are somewhat harder overall.
- The Tollkeeper grows much deadlier as the fight goes on: each toll now raises its damage by a quarter (was a tenth), and its rings hit a third harder at the first toll and about two thirds harder by the third.
- The Tollkeeper starts the fight much weaker: it opens at half damage, rings included, and climbs back to its old strength over its first two tolls. It tolls every 12 seconds instead of 10, and its flail between tolls is halved again, so its melee starts at a quarter of what it was.
- Surge of Blood now gives its 25% chance of a Fervour charge on kills while it is up, as its card says; before, the chance never applied.
- Several bonuses that only apply under a condition were being ignored in some places, and now work: flask charges gained on a kill, life gained on a kill per charge held, extra item quantity and rarity on low life, and the damage of Searing Mantle's burn, Cinder Herald, Cinder Blow's burst, Cinder Bond and the Sour Herald crawler.
- Rampage Call's card now says what rage is: you gain it from melee hits when a skill or support gives it, such as Fury Well or Snag Lash, up to 50, and it fades a few seconds after you stop hitting.
- The Skills tab gem list now updates at once when you socket or remove a gem, instead of only after you switch tabs.
- Fixed stray lines running to the corner of the screen from the outlines of area attacks and warnings on some phones.
- The outlines of enemy area warnings and lasting ground zones are thinner and fainter, so they clutter the screen less.
- Through the first 20 maps, gems of your class's main attribute drop much more often, so your early gems fit your class: about 60 to 70 percent of gem drops, up from about 40 to 55 percent.

- A skill set to the Escape role on the Strategy tab has two boxes, Low health and Surrounded, so a blink can go only when life is low, only when enemies crowd in, or both. Before, it always went when three enemies were close, even at full life. The life threshold now goes from 10% to 80%.
- The main menu has a Patch notes screen.
- A support that can do nothing for a skill is no longer offered for it. One already socketed stays, greyed out with the reason, and no longer adds mana cost or lowers damage.

- Echoing Cast no longer works on totems or brands, Wide Crush needs a skill with an area, and Kindle and Cindering, which were the same gem, are now one (a Cindering you own becomes a Kindle).
- New support: Flanking Volley, the attack version of Flanking Cast, for Arrow Shower and Ember Downpour.
- Bosses can no longer be out-healed: map regeneration no longer reaches them, and a boss's own healing pauses after it is hit. The Courser has about 40% more life, hits about 80% harder and gallops further.
- The Weakest target priority now picks the enemy with the least life left, so a boss's escort usually falls before the boss.
- The passive tree has many more ways through: clusters link to related neighbours, roads are mostly attribute nodes, starts join straight onto their two starter clusters, and plain increased damage is gone. Passives are refunded again.
- Areas now show the exact ground they cover, outlined in the colour of their damage. Attacks fly as arrows and spells as orbs, with a shadow and a trail, and monsters' projectiles have a red rim.
- Every skill with a cooldown shows it once on its card. Standards did not show theirs, and blinks and Jaws Trap showed it two or three times.
- A theme's champion now ends an ordinary map only about one time in four, and the map offers no longer announce it.

## 2026-10-10

### Save slots

- Every run now has its own save. Starting a new run no longer replaces the one you had.
- The title screen lists your saved runs, most recently played first, as "Continue — class, level, map". Each has
  Copy (puts the save on the clipboard for a bug report) and Delete (asks first).
- A run that ends in death or victory, or that you abandon, deletes its own save and no other. "Save and quit" keeps it.
- A save from before this patch is picked up automatically and shows in the list.
- A save from an older, incompatible version shows as its own row that you can discard.
- If the browser has no room left to save, the camp says so; delete an old save on the title screen to make room.
- There is no limit on the number of saves, but browsers keep about 5 MB for the whole game, so a long list of deep runs can
  fill it.

### Evasion and accuracy now matter

Monsters' evasion and accuracy now grow with level much faster than before. Until now monster evasion grew in a straight line,
far too slowly, so characters hit nearly always and monsters hit characters nearly always, whatever either had invested.

- Your hit chance falls as you go deeper unless you build accuracy. A character in plain gear used to hit 100% of the time at
  every level; it now hits about 92% at level 30, 85% at level 60, 72% at level 84 and 63% at level 100. The Hit chance on the
  Character page shows it against a plain monster of the map's level.
- Accuracy is a real stat: accuracy on weapons, gloves, helmets, rings and quivers, and Dexterity, buy hits back.
- Evasion works: monsters used to hit even a well-evaded character almost every time. Now evasion turns away a
  fair share of hits: a plain-geared level 60 character is hit about 78% of the time, and one with more
  evasion than that, less.
- Monster life is adjusted for it: evasive monsters (gloomstalkers, gnawers, cutpurses) have a few percent less life, since
  they are now harder to hit.

Known issues: an evasion character at levels 10 to 30 is hit 10 to 15 points more often than intended, because armour bases
grow in steps.

### Monsters and combat

- Rare monsters have more than two and a half times the life they had and mini-bosses about four and a half times, so they take much longer to bring down.
- Deeper maps hold more magic and rare monsters: by map 60 about one pack in five is a rare pack, up from one in twelve, and magic packs and the monsters around a rare gain one more magic monster every 25 maps.
- Many monsters now have a second attack. Ranged and ground attacks are used while the character is out of melee reach, or on a
  timer: thrown bone spears, lobs of bile, boulders, slowing chains, rings of spores. The Slinger's stones slow the character.
- New telegraphed blast patterns: March, Mortar, Cross, Toll waves, Closing ring, Keening and Twin hooks. Each blast shows its
  warning shape before it lands, and a later step in a pattern is drawn faintly until its warning begins. The death recap names
  the pattern.
- Defensive windows: several monsters now turn aside or soften hits for a short time, and each shows a tell. The Shieldbearer's
  Brace turns aside hits from its front. The Gilded Guard's Counter-stance answers melee blows. The Choirmaster's Sanctuary
  shelters allies while it sings, and a stun silences it. The Sentinel's Aegis turns aside four hits and regrows when left alone.
  The Gloomstalker fades out of reach when burst down. The Charnel Heap turns to stone, mends, then shatters in a ring. The Bone
  Beetle curls up and takes far less damage until fire or a stun opens it. Damage over time still works through all of these.
- Mode shifts: some monsters change behaviour mid-fight (Golem: Core vent. Slag Brute: Quench. Gorger: Gorged. Hag: Last
  rites. Wailer: Grieving. Rend-boar: Riled. Cinderling: Molten. A stun breaks the guard of the Shieldbearer and the Sentinel).
  There is a new Adaptive rare modifier.
- New debuffs, shown on the HUD as Blinded, Marked and Tithed. The Bursar's Tithe drains the character's life through a beam.
  The Handler's whistle marks the character, and its pack hits 20% harder for 4 seconds. The Hexer's hex is now a sigil on the
  ground.
- New pack plays: archers and slingers hold, then loose together down lanes shown first. Three or more hounds close a ring around
  the character, then all leap. A Nest drives its brood in faster and harder. A hound waits for its pack's ring instead of
  leaping alone.
- Map cards show two new tags, Patterns and Guards, and the threat estimate counts second attacks.

### Skills and items

- Lone Prism gave mana on every hit, including spells. It now gives mana only for hits by attacks, as intended. Hail Bow and
  Anchorfall Blade had the same problem and are fixed.
- Chained Charges and Heavy Charge cost far less mana (30% of the skill's cost instead of 150%), so mine setups are no longer
  the most expensive way to run a spell.
- Skills tab and gear comparisons: a blink, guard or buff skill in the loadout no longer makes the main skill's damage and mana
  sustain look far worse than they are. Rime Step, Cinder Step and Surge of Blood all had this problem.

### Strategy

- New Strategy tab in camp. Each skill gets a role (main, periodic, keep-up, opener, emergency, automatic, off) and a condition
  (any, packs, few enemies, rares, bosses), and the character uses them in that order.
- The same tab sets the target priority (including sticking to the current target), spacing and flask thresholds.
- It also shows the rotation's damage against a pack and a boss, and the Character sheet shows both when they differ.
  Comparisons weigh pack damage 70% and boss damage 30%.
- A periodic skill no longer needs the main skill's reach. It fires when its own target is in reach and waits out its pause after
  a use. It also no longer stands in for a main skill that is waiting for mana, so the weapon attacks while mana comes back.
- The skill bar lists every main and damage skill.
- New Escape role for blinks and run buffs (Quick Step, Cinder Step, Rime Step, Bolt Step, Rotting Stride, Shadow Swap, Smoke Charge,
  Shadow Quiver, Slipstream Run). An escape skill is used when life is below its threshold (70% by default, 30% to 70%) with an enemy
  within 5 tiles, or when 3 or more enemies are within 4 tiles. A blink with this role goes to the safest spot in its reach,
  away from the enemies.

### Interface

- Reward offers were showing as plain grey browser buttons, which made the cyan gem names hard to read. They look right again.

## 2026-10-09

### Skill balance

- Spirit Saw was far too strong: against a single enemy it did about three times the damage of any other spell. Its direct hit is
  now small, and its chaos debuff has been scaled down to sit near the middle of the spells. It still pierces, homes in on
  enemies and hits everything it passes, so it remains best in a crowd, but it no longer wins on a lone target.
- Other damage-over-time skills had the same problem and have been rebalanced the same way: Sap Bolt, Acid Arrow, Creeping
  Plague, Searing Lance, Venom Shower, Ruin Ritual, Cinder Bond and Drain Trap. Sap Bolt, Ruin Ritual, Cinder Bond and Searing
  Lance get weaker, and Creeping Plague gets stronger. Venom Shower and Acid Arrow change little.
- Storm Rush was doing about five times the damage of a normal melee attack. The waves of lightning along the illusion's path
  now hit for one normal attack each, or two and a half times that once the illusion has stopped. Only the final wave, where you
  join the illusion, grows with the number of stages you built. Storm Rush is still one of the stronger melee skills, but no
  longer by a wide margin.

### Items and crafting

- The Items screen has been rebuilt. It shows three columns on a desktop (gear, the selected item, your bag), two on a tablet and
  a single sheet on a phone. You can search, combine two rows of filters, tidy your bag from a menu, see how full it is, and drag
  items to equip or remove them.
- Selecting an item now shows a comparison for every slot it could go into, with what would change before and after.
- Confirmations (Items, Clean up, Abandon) now use one in-game panel instead of the browser's pop-up.
- The Workbench now shows every craft before you pay for it, grouped by what you are trying to achieve. Each option shows its
  cost, its odds and what it would change on your character. Crafts that roll show a typical result and the range, never the
  actual roll. Options you can't use show the reason beside them, and the result panel shows before and after.

### Combat and maps

- Ranged monsters now keep walking toward your character and only shoot a minion after they have been held up for 1.5 seconds.
  Before, they stopped and shot the first minion they saw.
- Cinder Bond's beams are now drawn on screen.
- Totems are placed with some randomness where their beams cross enemies, and a full set of totems only counts those near your
  character.

### Interface

- The passive tree tooltip now appears next to the cursor and stays on screen.
- The title screen no longer has demo buttons. The checkbox for anonymous results now uses the game's accent colour.

### Known issues

- Searing Mantle, Surge of Blood, and the running behaviour of Rotting Stride and Slipstream Run are new and still being
  balance-tested. They may feel too strong or too weak.
- Withering Breath and Cinder Step's burning trail are due for a balance pass.

### Skills rework

Nearly every skill has been reworked so that it behaves the way its description says. If a skill felt off to you before, try
it again.

- Curses are now cast by your character, last their full duration, and your curse limit applies.
- Warcries, banners, guard skills and stances follow their own rules, and each card explains them.
- Channelled skills build up stages while you hold them and release them at the end.
- Traps, mines and totems are placed and triggered the way their cards describe.
- Projectile skills spread, pierce, chain and fork the way their originals do.
- Corpses matter: some skills raise, explode or use up the bodies of dead monsters.
- Ailments and damage over time are now debuffs of their own.
- Every special skill's card now says what the skill actually does.
- Viper Lash is now a poison projectile that flies from enemy to enemy. It was an instant lightning-style chain before.
- Mirror Quiver's clone now fires with your own bow.
- Rain of Steel fires several volleys, each wider and weaker than the one before.
- Rime Drift is now a skull that bursts into chilling ground, which creeps toward enemies.
- Brute Force: melee hits now carry the extra poison and bleed damage from your gear.
- Standards (banners): the stages you build up now make the buff you receive stronger too, not only the penalty on enemies.

### Removed

- These gems have been removed: Ghost Coil, Rot Drain Trap, Cluster Bolt and Festering Crit.
- Wake Blades, Wake Sentinel and the other skills that animated weapons have been removed as well, along with the uniques that depended on them: Marshal's Hauberk, Bladewake Hammer, Whirlwind Falchion and Twinwhirl Falchion.

### How your character fights

- Rotting Stride and Slipstream Run now behave like running skills. When your character lands among a pack (Rotting Stride), or
  when a crowd closes in or you are hurt (Slipstream Run), it runs on past the enemies without attacking until the effect is
  over. It does not run when it only uses Rotting Stride to reach one enemy.
- Searing Mantle and Orbiting Blades: a character that wears the Mantle or has blades circling it now walks in among enemies, so
  the fire and the blades actually reach them.
- Searing Mantle goes out when your life falls below 40 percent and lights again at 75 percent.

### Maps and play

- Offer cards now show one table with the same rows on every card: item quantity, item rarity, experience, monster counts and
  bonus loot.
- Your character no longer dodges a monster's attack by running through the monster. That used to send it back and forth instead
  of fighting.
- A map that has run 500 seconds now starts the escape timer by itself, so a stuck character no longer sits until the timeout
  kills it. You keep the loot and experience, but get no clear reward.
- Fixed a crash while building certain maps.
