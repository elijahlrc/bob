# Bob — Audit of the armour and jewellery affixes against PoE 3.9

Status: **audit done 2026-10-16; the fix pass followed the same day (next section).** It follows [AUDIT-WEAPON-AFFIXES.md](AUDIT-WEAPON-AFFIXES.md) and, with it, covers every
affix that can appear on a droppable item in Bob except flasks (section 10). The sections after the fix pass describe the game as it was on the day of the
audit. Reference text and numbers live only in this file and under `docs/coverage/` (DESIGN §3); nothing here goes in `src/`.

## The fix pass (2026-10-16)

The author asked for parity with 3.9 and took every recommendation of section 10. What was built:

- **A family for each ladder.** The armour and jewellery mods are `src/data/armourAffixes.ts` (157 families, generated once from the 3.9 tables and
  then hand-edited source, like `weaponTables.ts`). A mod whose ladder differs by slot is several families of one `group` (life has five), and an
  item rolls only one of them. Tier levels, ranges and relative weights are the reference's; the top tier is still a quarter as likely as the lowest.
  The pool sizes now match 3.9 less the omitted mods (the regression test lists them), and the relative weights are the reference's (resistances lost
  their old 1.4 and life its 1.5, which had made up for the smaller pools).
- **Hybrids and defence types.** A pure piece rolls only its own set of defences; an armour-and-evasion piece rolls the pair mods (with the
  armour-and-life style hybrids at half weight). Attributes follow the type (`hybrid`, `arShield`, `evShield` and `esShield` are new item tags).
  **Correction to the audit:** headline 3 and section 2 said shields roll no attributes; the generated appendix (and the game) has them roll the
  attribute of their type, which is what was built.
- **New mods:** hybrid flat defences, defence with life or mana, defence with reduced stun duration on you (a pair of defences is one roll that
  feeds both stats: `ModTemplate.same`; so is the shield's attack and cast speed), reflect, additional physical damage reduction, dodge, spell block, avoid elemental ailments
  (`avoid.elemental`), life regeneration percent, reduced attribute requirements, minion and melee gem levels, the spirit-shield mods, quiver life,
  resistances and projectile speed and damage over time multiplier, kill gains and mana leech, typed damage on rings and amulets, amulet percent
  defences, belt armour and flask mods, ring evasion, enemy stun threshold on belts, stun duration on belts and quivers. Movement speed is the
  boots prefix `bootsMoveSpeed`.
- **Numbers** are the 3.9 numbers per slot. Flat armour and evasion against Bob's own bases now: body 400 on 480 (0.8, was 0.67), helmet 138 on
  240 (0.6), gloves and boots 60 on 168 (0.36), a shield 400 on 288 (1.4); the reference has 0.5, 0.4, 0.25 and about 0.8 (a shield base of 522 at level 64). Bob's bases are lighter than the reference's, so a flat roll is
  a bigger part of the piece; lowering the bases is the lever if it proves too strong.
- **Retired, not deleted** (items that have them keep them, they roll nowhere): the five ailment chances on gloves, added chaos damage to
  attacks, socketed gem level on armour, area of effect, aura effect, reduced mana reserved, the wearer's stun threshold and the old movement
  speed suffix. Avoid being stunned is an armour-shield mod, the weapon-shared families (mana, elemental damage with attacks, critical
  multiplier, life on hit, stun duration, mana regeneration, the attributes) are weapon-only and their armour versions are new families.
- **Omitted** for want of an engine stat: block of projectile attacks, life and mana gained when you block, percent energy shield regeneration,
  reduced flask charges used, block recovery (the stun half of the hybrids is reduced stun duration on you). Item rarity and the light radius half of
  the accuracy hybrid do not apply.
- **Bases.** Shields come in all six defence types (a round shield has no implicit: block recovery has no stat), every shield type has its 3.9
  implicit scaled to four tiers (the first tower shield has none, as in the reference), and rings, amulets, belts and quivers have a base for each
  kind of implicit the reference has (55 jewellery bases, from 14). A jewellery base is a kind and not a tier: any one the item level allows is
  as likely as another.
- **Card and wording.** The card shows the piece after its mods, with the changed numbers coloured (`armourLine`; a shield's block mod is local,
  so it counts). The lines read as the reference's: "Regenerate N Life per second", "+N% Chance to Block", "N% of Physical Attack Damage Leeched
  as Life", "N% increased Armour and Evasion", "Elemental Damage with Attack Skills", "N% chance to Avoid being Stunned". "Critical Strike
  Multiplier" and "Gain N Life per Enemy Hit with Attacks" keep Bob's own wording, as in the weapon pass.

Not done: the flasks (their own affix pool), and uniques are covered by the coverage ledger.

## Scope, sources and method

**What counts.** Everything that can put a line on a helmet, gloves, boots, body armour, shield, ring, amulet, belt or quiver: the prefix and
suffix families of `src/data/affixes.ts` whose slots reach one of them, and the base implicits of `src/data/bases.ts`. Bob has six defence
types (armour, evasion, energy shield and the three pairs), so each of the four armour slots is run six times and the shield three times.

**Rules already settled** (weapon audit, section 10): crafting-only routes (bench, essences, Rot Seal, quality) are ignored; the top tier of an
affix is deliberately a quarter as likely as the lowest, not what the reference game does; items the player already has keep their affixes;
numbers are Bob's own and only reported where they change what a mod is for or how often it appears.

**Bob's side.** `FAMILY_DEFS`, eligibility by `itemTags` / `eligible` (`src/gen/loot.ts`), tiers as `expandFamily` builds them, weights as
`rollAffix` sums them, run at item level 84 for the top tier of each base (`*_4`, and `ring_fire`, `amulet_str`, `belt_life`, `quiver_phys`).

**The reference side.** The same source as the weapon audit: Path of Building v1.4.155 `Data/3_0/ModItem.lua` (tag of 2019-12-14, the last
release before 3.10), kept outside the repo, and the base data in `docs/coverage/pob-bases.json`. A mod rolls on an item when the first of its
weight tags the item has gives a weight above zero. The tag sets for the classes here:

- armour pieces: the slot (`helmet`, `gloves`, `boots`, `body_armour`), the defence type (`str_armour`, `str_dex_armour`, ..., never a
  three-way type, which Bob does not have), `armour`, `default`;
- shields: `shield`, the shield type (`str_shield`, `str_dex_shield`, ...), the defence type, `armour`, `default`; the pure energy shield
  shield (a spirit shield) carries `focus` instead of a type;
- ring, amulet, belt, quiver: the slot and `default`.

**Limits.**

- The tag sets are inferred from the weight keys; the files do not say which tags a base carries. The shield rows are the least certain:
  without the defence-type tag a shield rolls no local percent defence at all, which is not how the game plays, so the type tag is assumed.
- Left out of the 3.9 side: influence mods (Shaper, Elder and the four Conqueror/Hunter tags), corrupted implicits, mods with weight zero on a
  plain item, and the 35 talisman amulets (their implicits are a league feature).
- The audit counts what the roll rules produce. It ignores mod groups blocking each other and the cap of three prefixes and three suffixes.

## Headline

1. **Bob's armour and jewellery pools are smaller, and life is a bigger share of them.** A body armour rolls 4 prefixes and 10 suffixes in Bob,
   6 and 9 in 3.9 (hybrids 7 and 11); a shield 3 and 11 against 7 to 21 and 14 to 15; a belt 3 and 10 against 8 and 12; an amulet 11 and 17
   against 15 and 22. Of the 103 distinct mod texts that can roll on these items in 3.9, 39 have a Bob family with the same stat (not always on the same slot); 61 have
   none, and 3 do not apply (item rarity twice, and the light radius half of one accuracy hybrid). Section 3.
2. **Hybrid defence pieces roll the wrong set.** In 3.9 a piece that gives armour and evasion rolls only the "armour and evasion" mods. In Bob
   it rolls those and also the pure armour and the pure evasion mods, so five of its seven prefix families are defence, and one item can carry
   flat armour, percent armour and percent evasion together. Section 2.
3. **Attributes ignore the defence type.** Bob rolls Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence on every piece, shields included. In 3.9 a body
   armour rolls only the attribute of its type, helmets also roll Intelligence, gloves also Dexterity, and shields the attribute of their type
   (the first draft said none; the generated appendix shows otherwise, see the fix pass). Section 2.
4. **Jewellery copies the weapon numbers where 3.9 has smaller ones.** Amulet spell damage tops at 89% against 26%, amulet cast speed at 29%
   against 13% (ring 29% against a single 5-7% tier), life per hit at 10 against 2 (ring, gloves) and 4 (amulet), leech 1.0% of all damage from
   item level 6 against 0.2-0.4% of physical damage only from item level 50. Section 5.
5. **Flat defences have no slot scale.** Flat armour or evasion tops at 320 on every armour piece. In 3.9 that is 400 on a body armour, 138 on a
   helmet and 60 on gloves and boots. On a Bob glove it is almost twice the base's own armour; in 3.9 it is a quarter. Section 6.
6. **Eleven Bob families have no 3.9 counterpart on these items**, among them the +1 and +2 socketed gem levels on helmets and body armour,
   area of effect, aura effect, reduced mana reserved, all five ailment chances on gloves and added chaos damage to attacks; nine more roll
   on slots where 3.9 does not roll them. Section 4.
7. **The engine already has most of what is missing.** Block of spells, additional physical damage reduction, dodge, reflect, avoid ailments,
   life on block (as a percent), damage over time multiplier, projectile speed, kill gains and the gem level stats are all calculated. Only block of
   projectiles, mana on block, percent energy shield regeneration and block recovery are not. Section 3.
8. **The item card shows an armour piece's base defences, not the piece.** A helmet with +300 armour and 90% increased armour prints its white
   base's number. The weapon card was fixed on the same day. Section 8.
9. **The base inventory is much thinner.** Bob has 4 armour tiers per defence type where 3.9 has 8 to 17, no hybrid shields, no shield
   implicits (nearly every 3.9 shield has one) and 5 rings, 4 amulets, 3 belts and 2 quivers against 25, 14, 10 and 14. Section 9.
10. **Tier odds are not a problem here.** 3.9 weights nearly every armour and jewellery tier alike (1000 each; a few top tiers 500), so the top
    three tiers are 48% of the rolls at item level 84, against 34% in Bob with the quarter rule. This is information only, the rule stands.

## 2. Defence type and hybrids

**Hybrid pieces.** 3.9 gives each defence type its own set. A pure armour piece rolls flat armour, percent armour, percent armour with stun
and block recovery, and flat armour with life. A piece that gives armour and evasion rolls only the pair versions of the same four, never
the single-defence ones. Bob gates its families by `requires`, which is an AND of the tags the item has, so an armour-and-evasion piece
passes every `requires: ['ar']` and every `requires: ['ev']` family as well as `requires: ['ar', 'ev']`:

| Bob family     | Armour piece: 3.9 / Bob | Armour-and-evasion piece: 3.9 / Bob |
| -------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `armourLocal`  | rolls / rolls           | does not / rolls                    |
| `incArmour`    | rolls / rolls           | does not / rolls                    |
| `evasionLocal` | does not / does not     | does not / rolls                    |
| `incEvasion`   | does not / does not     | does not / rolls                    |
| `incArEv`      | does not / does not     | rolls / rolls                       |

So a hybrid body armour has life, five defence families and the rare gem level in a seven-family prefix pool, and its defence is 72% of
the prefix weight (12% to 16% each for the five). In 3.9 it is life, the pair mod, the pair mod with stun recovery, flat pair, two flat
hybrids with life and reflect, and the three pair mods are 49% of the weight (58% with the hybrids with life). The other two pairs (armour
with energy shield, evasion with energy shield) behave the same.

**Attributes.** In 3.9 the attribute suffixes follow the type of the piece:

- Strength: pieces with an armour component (armour, armour and evasion, armour and energy shield); rings, amulets, belts;
- Dexterity: pieces with an evasion component, gloves of every type; rings, amulets, quivers;
- Intelligence: pieces with an energy shield component, helmets of every type; rings, amulets;
- shields roll the attribute of their type, like the other pieces.

Bob rolls all three on every armour piece, shield, ring and amulet, Strength on belts and Dexterity on quivers. An evasion body armour can
offer Strength in Bob and cannot in 3.9.

**Mana on armour.** In 3.9 maximum mana rolls on helmets, gloves, boots and body armour that carry energy shield, on energy shield
shields, rings, amulets and belts. Bob rolls it on helmets, gloves and boots of every type, never on a body armour or a shield. Armour and
evasion pieces roll it in Bob and cannot in 3.9, and the body armour and shield cases are missing.

**Ladders.** 3.9 life runs 8 to 13 tiers, ending at item level 44 on a ring, 54 on gloves, boots and an amulet, 64 on a helmet, belt and
quiver, 73 on a shield and 86 on a body armour. Bob has nine tiers ending at 80 on every slot. A ring's and amulet's added damage runs 6 to

**Weights (information).** The weight of a 3.9 tier is 1000 on almost everything here (500 for some, for example attack speed on gloves), so
a mod's weight in its pool is about its number of tiers at the item level: mana 11 to 13, life 8 to 13, resistances 8, attributes 9 or 10,
kill gains 3, leech 1 to 3, flat added damage 4 to 9. Bob's families carry a weight of one to one and a half (life x1.5, the three resistances
x1.4, movement speed x1.5), spread over fewer tiers with the quarter rule; the shares are in section 1.

## 6. Flat defences against the base

Bob's flat local armour, evasion and energy shield do not depend on the slot, while a slot's base does. Top bases: Bob's tier four against
the best 3.9 base (the 3.9 figure is the highest of its single-type bases up to item level 84).

| Slot   | Bob base (armour / evasion; energy shield) | Bob flat top (armour / evasion; energy shield) | 3.9 base        | 3.9 flat top |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------ |
| Body   | 480; 170                                   | 320; 75                                        | 776 to 854; 163 | 400; 100     |
| Helmet | 240; 85                                    | 320; 75                                        | 377 to 380; 76  | 138; 61      |
| Gloves | 168; 60                                    | 320; 75                                        | 242; 47         | 60; 49       |
| Boots  | 168; 60                                    | 320; 75                                        | 241 to 246; 49  | 60; 49       |

Flat armour or evasion is two thirds of a Bob body armour's base, 1.3 times a helmet's and 1.9 times a glove's or boot's; in 3.9 it is
0.5, 0.4 and 0.25 of them. Flat energy shield is roughly in line with 3.9 (0.4 to 1.25 of the base against 0.6 to 1.0). So the armour and
evasion flats make helmets, gloves and boots far more defence-heavy than their 3.9 equivalents, and a body armour comparatively light.

## 7. Calculation differences

1. **Hybrid selection** (section 2): the largest. It changes what an armour-and-evasion piece is worth and what it can roll together.
2. **Leech** counts all attack damage (`leech.life` with the attack tag and no damage type), where 3.9 leeches physical attack damage only.
   Combined with item level 6 and four tiers up to 1.0%, an elemental attacker leeches through a ring or glove at a rate no 3.9 item gives.
   The weapon pass built `lifeLeechPhys` / `manaLeechPhys` for weapons with the physical type; the jewellery family was not touched.
3. **Life per hit** is attack-only in both games (the weapon pass made it so) and the number is 2.5 to 5 times larger.
4. **Local defence maths** matches: (base + flat) x (1 + increased), shield block adds the local block mod, hybrids scale each defence.
5. **Stun threshold** is the wearer's own in Bob (the stat feeds `stunThreshold` in `defence.ts`); the 3.9 belt mod lowers the enemy's.
   Bob has the enemy-side stat already (`enemyStunThreshold`, weapons only). Moving it to belts is plain.
6. **Added damage to attacks** is attack-tagged in both games, so it applies only to attack hits, as it should.
7. **Accuracy** from helmets, gloves, rings, amulets and quivers is global in both games; Bob has no light radius to pair with it.
8. **Tier odds** (section 1 and headline 10) are by design.

## 10. A fix pass, and the decisions it needs

**Plain fixes** (no design call; the same kind as the weapon pass, to do on the author's go-ahead):

1. Hybrid pieces roll only the hybrid mods; pure pieces only the pure ones (`not` on the single-defence families, or tags for the types).
2. Attributes by defence type (Intelligence on every helmet, Dexterity on every glove, shields by their type); mana by defence type and on body
   armour and spirit shields.
3. Slot-scaled tables (`weaponTables.ts`-style) for life, mana, energy shield, flat armour and evasion, accuracy, added damage to attacks,
   all attributes; flat defences in proportion to the base.
4. Amulet spell damage, cast speed and ring speeds, life per hit and the jewellery leech to the 3.9 numbers, with physical-only leech from
   item level 50; ring attack and cast speed one tier; the amulet and ring weights.
5. Quiver life and resistances; belt flat armour, ring flat evasion, amulet percent defences; life and mana on kill and mana leech on
   gloves, rings, amulets and quivers; typed damage suffixes on rings and amulets; projectile speed, damage over time multiplier on quivers.
6. Engine-supported shield and body mods: reflect, additional physical damage reduction, dodge, spell block, avoid elemental ailments, life
   regeneration percent, and the hybrid flat defences.
7. Movement speed a prefix with the 3.9 ladder; stun duration on belts and quivers; enemy stun threshold on belts; critical strike chance
   for amulets and quivers only.
8. The item card shows the piece after its mods; the wording table of section 8.

**Decisions** (functional differences that change what the game offers; each with a recommendation):

1. **Bob's extras** (section 4). Recommendation: follow the weapon rulings and drop the five ailment chances on gloves, `addChaosAttacks`,
   `gemLevel` on armour and `stunThreshold`; move `stunAvoid` to armour shields, `stunDuration` to belts and quivers, `eleAttacks` to belts
   and `critGlobal` to amulets and quivers. `areaEffect`, `auraEffect` and `reservation` are the open ones: they have no 3.9 source but are
   what makes some builds, so keep or drop is yours.
2. **Stun and block recovery.** A hybrid in 3.9 on every armour type and a suffix on five slots. Bob has no block recovery; stun duration on
   you exists. Options: (a) build both as "reduced stun duration on you" and drop the block half, (b) leave them out and keep the plain
   defence mods. Recommendation (a).
3. **Missing engine stats**: block of projectile attacks, mana on block, percent energy shield regeneration, flat life on block. Recommendation:
   omit, as with the not-added weapon mods (the pool shrinks by four mods on shields and body armour).
4. **Belt flask mods** (five). The calculation has flask recovery rate, charges gained and effect duration; reduced charges used has no
   stat. Recommendation: add the other four; they give a belt an identity and flasks are part of the loop.
5. **Hybrid shields and shield implicits** (section 9). Recommendation: add the three hybrid shield types and give all shields their 3.9
   implicit, scaled to Bob's four tiers.
6. **Jewellery and quiver bases.** Recommendation: add the missing implicit kinds in the weapon pass's spirit (the item's reason to exist),
   about ten rings, amulets, belts and quivers more, with Bob names. This is the largest item by volume.
7. **Flat defences by slot.** Recommendation: use the 3.9 numbers per slot (section 6), as was done for flat weapon damage, and re-check
   the bases if gloves and boots become too weak a place to put armour.

**After the fix pass.** The items that remain unaudited are flasks (their own affix pool, `src/data/flasks.ts`) and uniques (covered by the
coverage ledger, not affix by affix). Everything that can roll on a droppable weapon, armour piece or jewel is then in these two audits.
